Intermediate Representation — the spine under canon

Not what you write, not what runs — the middle form where meaning lives and analysis happens.

canon · design · 2026-06-18 · the DAG is an IR · the folds are passes

An intermediate representation is the normalized middle form between a surface (what you write) and a target (what runs). The surface is sugar; the target is mechanics; the IR is where the meaning sits in a shape you can compute over. Compilers discovered the idea — but the reason it keeps reappearing is general, and it is the structural bet under all of canon. canon is not using an IR as a convenience. canon is an IR project, and seeing that makes the whole architecture click into one picture.

1 · What an IR buys — and why it keeps getting reinvented

Four properties, each load-bearing, none cosmetic:

PropertyWhat it buys
N×M → N+M Many frontends, many backends, one middle. LLVM's whole reason to exist: instead of every language targeting every chip (N×M translators), each side lowers to one IR and you write each side once (N+M). The economic argument for the entire idea.
normalization Many surface ways to say the same thing collapse to one canonical form — so an analysis is written once against the IR, not once per surface dialect.
passes compose Each transformation is IR → IR. Optimization, validation, annotation stack cleanly because they share an input and output shape. The pass pipeline is the program.
progressive lowering Not one IR but a stack at descending abstraction (MLIR's thesis: many dialects, lowered step by step). High-level IR for domain reasoning, low-level IR for execution — reason where it's cheap, execute where it's fast.

Prior art to inherit, not reinvent: LLVM IR (N×M→N+M), MLIR (dialects + progressive lowering), GCC GIMPLE, e-graphs / equality saturation (optimization as search over IR), tree-sitter (frontends), Substrait (a portable query plan as a backend-neutral IR — already on canon's emitter roadmap).

2 · The recognition — canon's authoritative object is an IR

Read the governing architecture back through this lens and the translation is exact:

Compiler IRcanon
the IR itself the lazy, content-addressed computation DAG — Entities (value-positions) and Activities (op-firings). A Merkle/IPLD graph; identical sub-DAGs share an id.
analysis / transform passes the folds — value, provenance, custody, validity, well-formedness, guarantee, confidence, temporal. Each is a ≤ₖ-monotone read of the same DAG into the Belnap carrier. A fold is a pass.
the stable interchange / "waist" the CID (content identifier). One self-describing address that is simultaneously node-id, prov:Entity IRI, and in-toto digest — the narrow waist every frontend and backend mates through.
pass-manager guarantees the acceptance test "add concern N without touching concern M" is ≤ₖ-monotonicity of each fold — a CI-checkable invariant, the formal version of "passes don't interfere."
This is the load-bearing reframe. "Every cross-cutting concern is a ≤ₖ-monotone fold over one content-addressed DAG" — canon's one structural idea — is the sentence "every concern is a pass over one IR." The independence of folds (the validation-fold family), the receipts-per-result (justified-verdict substrate), the four-valued algebra (Belnap carrier) are all consequences of choosing a content-addressed IR and writing the concerns as passes over it.

3 · Two altitudes — a stack of IRs, not one

canon already has the MLIR shape: two IRs at different altitudes, with lowering between them.

domain frontend          high IR                       low IR / target
─────────────────        ───────────────────────       ─────────────────────────
Sigma / CAR rule    →    detection-IR (motif graph)  →  emitted predicate   →  fires over
FIBO / ISO 20022    →    (molecule symbols, RDF)        (Python · SPARQL ·     the computation
(a future frontend) →                                   Rust · SPL)            DAG (the low IR)
  • The detection IR (motif/molecule ontology) is the high IR — domain logic as a graph of bonded gadgets (spawn ⋈ access on ProcessGuid), independent of any one rule language or query engine. The relation is the invariant; the language is a phrasebook.
  • The computation DAG is the low IR — the content-addressed substrate the emitted predicate actually fires over, and the object every fold reads. Provenance, custody, guarantee are passes here.
  • Lowering is progressive and verified: Sigma → motif graph → emitted predicate → firing. Because cross-language semantic equivalence is undecidable in general, each lowering step is fidelity-attested against a labeled corpus, not assumed — language-agnosticism is a measured property, not a wish (see the detection-IR page §3).

4 · Frontends and backends — why one core serves many domains

The N×M→N+M economics is exactly why canon's "domain-agnostic core + domain packages" works, and why the finance idea from the same conversation is coherent rather than a force-fit:

  • Frontends (N): a domain package lowers its surface into the IR. semantic-cyber lowers ATT&CK/D3FEND/Sigma; a hypothetical semantic-financial would lower FIBO / ISO 20022 / XBRL. They share a core because they lower to the same IR — not because finance resembles cyber.
  • Backends (M): emitters lower the IR out — the Python interpreter, a SPARQL knowledge surface, a Rust hot-path emitter (or, future-proofed, a Substrait plan over a vectorized engine). Each is fidelity-gated before it is trusted.
The test of a real IR is a second frontend. Canon's domain-agnostic claim is, today, proven by exactly one domain (cyber) — the same way its polyglot claim is proven by exactly one language binding (Python). A semantic-financial frontend would be the second domain that turns "agnostic" from a design intent into a measured property. The IR is the seam that makes that test cheap.

5 · The canon twist — the IR carries its own warrant

A normal compiler IR is discarded after lowering; nobody asks an add instruction to justify itself. canon's IR is content-addressed and tamper-evident, and one of its passes (the provenance fold) emits the justification as a peer object of the result. So the IR is not only the analysis surface — it is the audit trail. "How did you know?" has a content-addressed answer because the derivation is a walk of the same IR. That is the property generic IRs do not have, and the reason canon's IR is also its honesty machinery rather than a separate log.

6 · Honest scope — what's an IR here, and what an IR can't buy

  • Built: the computation DAG + the fold family (provenance Phases 0–4); the detection-IR first slice (detection/motif.py, two molecules, two emitters, agreement attested on the OTRF corpus). The structure of the stack is real.
  • Designed, not yet realized: the full molecule vocabulary (earned per ≥2 real detections, never speculated up front), additional emitters, and any second-domain frontend. A "stack of IRs" is the direction, not a finished tower.
  • An IR does not buy semantic equivalence. Structure ports because the graph is just data; semantics do not (ends_with byte-vs-Unicode, streaming-vs-materialized windows, null/timezone handling). The IR buys structural portability plus a verification harness — never guaranteed equivalence.
  • An IR is not free. You pay a serialization boundary and a normalization design cost. canon draws the core/periphery line exactly where that cost is worth it: tight folds over the live DAG stay in one runtime; per-result, offline artifacts cross the CID seam.
One line. An IR is one middle form that many surfaces lower into and many targets lower out of, with the real work written once as passes over it. canon's content-addressed DAG is that form; the folds are those passes; the CID is the waist — and because the IR is tamper-evident, it is also the warrant. The whole substrate is "compiler architecture, with provenance as a first-class pass."
