Entry, Attention, Fan-out: scaling the loop

The loop is the cell. Everything bigger — where canon enters an environment, what it checks first, many agents at once — is that same cell, fanned out and prioritized.

canon · design conversation · 2026-06-10 · continuation of abduction_loop.html · forward-looking, not built

The reassuring result: the three ways this gets bigger do not add a new primitive. They are the ground ⇄ stitch ⇄ test loop multiplied by two knobs.

scaled investigation = the loop × fan-out (scope) × priority (attention)

That factoring is the discipline as much as the design: build the cell before the fabric. A single loop producing one grounded narrative on one real Notable has to exist before entry-routing, learned attention, or multi-agent orchestration is worth a line of code.

1 · Entry points — which act of the loop dominates

Canon doesn't need a fixed integration; it attaches anywhere there's a claim to verify or a surface to ground against. But the entry point is not just "an integration" — it decides where in the loop you start, and therefore which act carries the weight.

SIEM-rich entry

The environment hands you TTP-tagged Notables, stock + built detections — given hypotheses. You enter near TEST: grade what's already proposed (the SIEM's own tag is just another untrusted claim). Verification-heavy; the LLM barely needed.

Raw-log entry

Nothing pre-interpreted. You enter at GROUND / STITCH: the battery surfaces shape anomalies and abduction builds the story from scratch. Generation-heavy; the abductive leap carries it.

So the environment's existing detection maturity shifts the balance between verification and abduction. Same loop, different starting node.

"Check everything that makes sense, given the environment + the log surface + the surfaced TTP" is computable, not a vibe. What is checkable =
  (telemetry layers present → the observability / NONE gate)
  ∩ (what the log surface affords → data-component coverage)
  ∩ (what the surfaced TTP routes to → W→technique).
That intersection is the frontier the loop enumerates over.

2 · Dynamic attention — what to check first, what's worth the cost

Over time, "what to check first" and "what's more valuable here" should surface on their own. That's a priority over the frontier:

priority = risk × information ÷ cost — conditioned on the environment

This is the cost-aware firing-order search the design notes already name (the regime-ledger learns it). One discipline, held firm:

Hand-coded cost-ordered greedy is the strong baseline. Greedy cheapest-test-per-bit is near-optimal for sequential testing. A learned attention mechanism earns its place only where non-greedy lookahead actually exists — where an expensive test A unlocks a cheap-but-decisive test B. Build the greedy version first, or you'll never know whether the learned one helped.

3 · Many agents — fan-out, but mind what "agent" means

The fan-out is real: independent devices / nodes investigate in parallel — the loop instantiated over the reachable frontier, two graph layers under it:

environment graph reachability / assets — "what can be reached from a compromised node" log graphs per-device provenance — the PROV graph for each node's own telemetry

Agents traverse both, per budget / intelligence. Parallel where independent, sequential only on the surface→verify→escalate spine.

"Many agents" should mean many parallel loop / verifier instances — not a swarm of LLMs. The whole through-line is to minimize the fluent-untrusted surface. A node's traversal is mostly graph + verifier (deterministic, cheap); the LLM is the scarce central resource, consulted only at the abductive junctions the verifier and the kill-chain graph can't make. One LLM call per frontier-region that needs a story — not one per device. An LLM-per-node swarm multiplies exactly the surface the architecture exists to shrink.

The honest ordering

  1. Entry points — most immediate. It's how the loop gets real data to run on, and both starting modes already have test shapes (OTRF/Mordor for raw-log, a SIEM Notable for TEST-entry).
  2. Dynamic attention — next layer up, with greedy-first as the rule. Don't build the learned version until the greedy one is the thing it must beat.
  3. Multi-agent orchestration — furthest out. The natural scale-out, and the easiest place to over-build before a single loop has produced one grounded narrative on one real Notable.
Sharpest next concrete step. Take one labeled OTRF/Mordor Notable, run the loop in TEST-entry mode (grade the given TTP), and watch what grounds vs what comes back NONE. That single run tells you whether the verification leg is real before investing in abduction, attention, or fan-out — and it reuses the OTRF first cell and the detection_verdict contract that already exist.
