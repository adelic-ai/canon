Retention & aging — what survives when the raw telemetry is gone

Don't keep the firehose. Decide what raw events fold into before they drop — and stay honest about what can no longer be reconstructed.

canon · design · 2026-06-19 · verdicts carry their own warrant · the horizon is a NONE boundary

A continuous monitor over a forgetful source (Endpoint Security / eslogger retains nothing) accumulates raw events without bound. You cannot keep the firehose. So the real question is not "how long do we keep raw events" but "what do raw events fold into before we drop them, and how do we stay honest about what we can no longer reconstruct." The source forgets immediately; retention is the workspace's job; and canon's edge over a normal SIEM's retention policy is two things a SIEM doesn't do — surviving verdicts carry their own justification, and the retention horizon is an honest NONE boundary, not a silent wall.

1 · The lifecycle — raw shrinks into derived

hot  (trailing window, recent raw)  ──fold──▶  warm (cells + baselines + verdicts)  ──age──▶  cold (verdicts + materialized provenance)
     the battery computes here                 raw rows dropped, statistics kept             re-derivation impossible → queries = NONE-bounded

Three things survive aging, in increasing compression:

SurvivorWhat it isWhy it survives
verdicts the justified outputs, content-addressed Only if justification is materialized, not pointed-at. A verdict that embeds its receipts (matched fields, W-record, the statistic) survives the raw events aging out; one that points into a log that later rolls becomes an unjustifiable assertion the moment the log is deleted.
baselines the fold of raw into "normal" — aggregate cells, not raw rows The battery's value is a calibrated model of normal. Keep the calibration (the FanoutCell per-(entity, time-bin) statistic); drop the events that produced it — another order of magnitude smaller, still enough to compute rarity.
the horizon a recorded coverage boundary Past it, re-derivation is impossible. A question about last month is a coverage NONE (telemetry aged out), never a clean FALSE — recorded, not a silent wall.

2 · Verdicts survive — but only if justification is materialized

The load-bearing architectural commitment: "justification is not metadata — it is the same object as the result." Operationally that becomes a retention rule. When a raw event is load-bearing for a verdict, its relevant fields are promoted into the verdict's provenance before the raw row becomes collectible. In content-addressed-DAG terms this is Merkle GC — pin-and-sweep, a named wheel:

  • verdicts are pin roots;
  • a raw node still reachable from a live verdict is pinned — not swept, even past the horizon;
  • raw nodes unreachable from any pin root and past the horizon are collectible.
The materialization-on-pin step is the one genuinely new mechanism. The rest — pin-and-sweep, windowed recompute — is named prior art. Promoting a load-bearing raw event's fields into the verdict before GC is what makes a verdict outlive its inputs without becoming an unjustifiable claim.

3 · Past the horizon — NONE, not FALSE

Once raw (and then cells) are gone you cannot re-derive or re-query that window. So the retention horizon is a first-class, recorded coverage boundary: "answerable back to date X; before X is NONE." A feed gap within the window (the monitor was down) is likewise a NONE for that window — and a live, unbroken feed is intact chain-of-custody, so feed-liveness = custody supplies the signal. This is the enterprise-ingest "fragmented retention → honest-NONE coverage" story at n=1.

4 · Aging is also desirable — and it is the flagged risk

Aging is not only storage management; it is relevance. You want the baseline to forget old behavior so "normal" tracks the current environment — a binary that was rare six months ago and is now ubiquitous should stop alarming. The sliding reference window is what keeps the calibration current. But this is precisely the architecture's flagged soft spot — conformal / baseline under distribution-shift and contaminated reference is unsolved:

HazardWhat happensMitigation
self-poisoning a slow-burn attacker who ramps gradually gets folded into "normal" as the window slides exclude flagged-anomalous and Both cells from baseline updates — don't let the thing you're hunting calibrate the hunter
drift false-alarms legitimate environment change reads as anomaly until the window catches up recorded as an assumption on the verdict; the per-result tier demotes when the reference window is suspect

Neither is solved. Both must be recorded assumptions on any baseline-derived verdict, not papered over.

5 · The Both queue — retained before it ages

The rare, contradictory (Both), or flagged cells are the highest-value things to retain longer and label — precisely because they are what the baseline would otherwise forget. They go to the active-learning queue before aging out. Aging a flagged cell silently into "normal" is the worst case; promoting it is the best.

6 · Honest scope

  • Built: nothing here. The streaming reader, the workspace store, and the GC/fold policy do not exist yet; this records the design before the build.
  • Depends on: the fold-then-drop step (raw → cells) needs the live source binding to exist first (live stream ingest).
  • Workspace config: retention horizons live in the workspace manifest ({hot_window, warm_horizon, cold_keeps_verdicts} per source) — multi-tenant by construction, different engagements keep different amounts.
One line. The source forgets immediately, so the workspace retains a shrinking, increasingly-derived trace (raw → cells → verdicts + materialized provenance); the horizon is an honest NONE boundary; and surviving verdicts carry their own justification, so they don't rot when their inputs do. This page is the outflow; live stream ingest is the intake.
