Three notions of "the same rule"

Detection needs three different "are these two rules the same?" tests — and the bugs live in the gap between what a rule says and what it does.

canon · design note · 2026-06-21 · the real structure behind clause_set / content_digest / catch_set

Three real, distinct equivalence relations on rules. They do not coincide, and each is the right tool for a different job.

The three "same?" tests

TestMechanismWhat it means
Same conditionsclause_setsearch for the same (field, predicate, value) atoms. The lattice's ⊆/∩ math → "broader / overlaps / neighborhood." Blind to filters and to AND-vs-OR wiring.
Same constructioncontent_digestbyte-identical logic — same atoms, filters, wiring. Strictest. Same conditions but different filter, or OR vs AND, differ here.
Same behaviorcatch-setfire on the same labeled instances. The only one about what a rule does, not what it says. Needs real attack data.

They're genuinely different — two failure cases prove it

  • Same conditions, different behavior (over-grouping): two svchost.exe rules, identical positive conditions, different filters (one excludes "in System32," the other "parent = services.exe") → catch opposite events. Conditions say same; behavior says different.
  • Different conditions, same behavior (under-grouping): the comsvcs pair — CallTrace contains comsvcs vs StartModule endswith comsvcs, totally different conditions → catch the same LSASS dump. Conditions say unrelated; behavior says synonyms.

The structure — a refinement order

Same-construction is the finest: content_digest-equal ⟹ same conditions and same behavior (identical logic forces both). Same-conditions and same-behavior are incomparable — neither implies the other; the two failure cases are exactly the two directions they come apart.

                 same construction  (content_digest — finest; implies both below)
                /                \
        same conditions      same behavior
          (clause_set)        (catch-set)
       what the rule SAYS    what the rule DOES    ← incomparable; this gap is where the bugs live

ground_lattice maps where "says" and "does" disagree

It cross-tabs the structural relation (same-conditions, via clause_set) against the behavioral relation (same-behavior, via catch-set) for every rule pair, and surfaces the two off-diagonals — over-grouped (conditions same, behavior different) and under-grouped (behavior same, conditions different). The comparison is irreducible: neither view alone tells you what the other does.

It uses clause_set for its structural axis on purpose — the lossy "same-conditions" view is exactly what over-grouping exposes. Using content_digest there would make over-grouping impossible to see (identical construction can't behave differently).

SKOS and FCA operate on the structural axis — at different resolutions

The "same conditions" side isn't one thing — it's a ladder of structural keys, coarse to fine, and SKOS and FCA just pick a rung:

FCA field-set  →  clause_set / SKOS  →  content_digest
value-blind        (field,pred,value)     everything
coarsest            graded relations      finest
(32 macOS → 1)     (filter-blind still)  (true dedup)        all on the "says" axis ───►
  • FCA — field-set key (coarsest, value-blind): groups rules by which fields they read, ignoring values. Maximally over-collapsing (32 distinct macOS detections → 1 concept).
  • SKOS — clause_set (finer): the graded order on (field, predicate, value) atoms — subset → broad/narrow, intersection → related, content_digest gating exact. Still positive-only → still filter/keyword-blind.
  • content_digest (finest): everything — the true-duplicate key.
Two consequences: coarser key ⟹ more over-grouping (FCA worst, SKOS less, content_digest none — the 32→1 and filter-blind-"exact" over-collapses are the structural axis over-claiming at the chosen resolution); and all of it is still "says," so all of it is grounded by catch-set — FCA concepts, SKOS relations, exact classes, none tell you what a rule does. Same ladder as the detection resolution axis tag → FCA field-set → content_signature → catch-set.

Which view for which job

  • Same-conditions (clause_set) — the lattice's relationships and neighborhoods (subsumption, overlap). The intent isn't dedup; it's the intersections — and a set is what you do that math on.
  • Same-construction (content_digest) — true duplicate detection, and the structure-aware exactMatch edges (so OR-vs-AND with the same conditions isn't a "synonym").
  • Same-behavior (catch-set) — the ground truth you validate the other two against.
The one durable point. The bugs live in the gap between what a rule says and what a rule does. That's why you can't pick a single view: the structural one scales and gives you the relationships, the behavioral one is the truth, and the comparison between them (ground_lattice) is the irreducible step that catches where they part.
