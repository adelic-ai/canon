Reading sift — the terms under the scanner

A glossary to scrutinize what the code actually does, not that it runs.

sift · Impressions on Data Security Engineering · a materialized sketch · detect · score · decide

sift is a materialized sketch: an idea about data-security detection made concrete and runnable, not a hardened product. It scans files for PII and secrets, and instead of thresholding each pattern match in isolation it treats the scan as thousands of hypothesis tests at once — is this match real, or a coincidence? — and controls the false-discovery rate across them. This page pins down the ~two-dozen load-bearing terms in that pipeline so the mechanism can be scrutinized, not taken on faith.

Legend. ⚠ load-bearing — conceptually subtle; where a misunderstanding or an overclaim would live. ○ mechanical — just needs a crisp definition.

1 · The three layers

Everything below hangs off one pipeline. Keeping the layers separate is the whole design — a detector is not a decision, and a score is not a p-value.

files → detect candidates (regex · checksum · entropy)
      → score each: a coincidence statistic (base × context)
      → decide: Benjamini–Hochberg over the scores, per document
      → verdict + coverage (including what could not be read)

The idea sift carries: detection, evidence-scoring, and the global decision are three separable things. A regex finds candidates; a coincidence score ranks them; a multiple-testing procedure decides which survive. Swap any layer without touching the others.

2 · Monte-Carlo, permutation, Phipson–Smyth — untangled

Are we permuting p-values? No — and that phrase hides three separate ideas.

  • Monte-Carlo null. To know how a statistic behaves by chance, you need its distribution under H₀. Rather than derive it with math, you sample it: draw many null examples, compute the statistic on each. In sift the benign corpus is the sampler — every detector firing on benign data is one draw of the statistic under "this is a coincidence."
  • Permutation null is a special case of a Monte-Carlo null where the null draws come from shuffling the labels of real data (valid because, under H₀, the labels are exchangeable). sift has no labels to shuffle — it samples a benign population instead. Same family, different null-generating mechanism.
  • Phipson–Smyth is not "the permutation method." It is a one-line correction for turning any finite empirical null into a p-value. The naive estimate b/N (fraction of null draws at least as extreme) is biased and can hit 0 — an invalid p-value, because you merely failed to sample far enough into the tail. The fix is (b+1)/(N+1). That +1 is the whole content, and it is exactly NullModel.pvalue: (1 + #{T_null ≤ t}) / (1 + N).

So: sift does a sampled (Monte-Carlo) empirical null, not a permutation, and borrows Phipson–Smyth's +1 because it applies to any finite null. The sampling happens on the data, to build the null distribution of the statistic; the p-value is read off only at the end. Nothing shuffles p-values.

One knot lives right here: the test statistic T sift calibrates is itself the heuristic "p-value" (base × context). So two objects both look p-ish — the heuristic score (a ranking statistic, not valid) and the calibrated empirical p-value (the valid one). Keep them distinct.

The decision layer — statistics

p-value⚠ P(a statistic at least this extreme | H₀ true). sift's heuristic base × context scores are not this; the calibrated empirical ones are. sits.py / calibrate.py
null hypothesis H₀⚠ Here: "this match is a coincidence, not real sensitive data." Everything is defined relative to it — change H₀ and every p-value changes.
super-uniform / valid p-value⚠ P(p ≤ α | H₀) ≤ α for every α. The property Benjamini–Hochberg requires; correct ordering alone does not supply it. This is the crux of the whole calibration. proven in test_calibrate.py

q — the FDR level○ The target false-discovery rate you set (0.05). The one dial on the decision.
test statistic (T)⚠ The scalar you rank and threshold on. sift reuses the heuristic score as T — smaller = stronger evidence — then recalibrates it. calibrate.py
multiple testing⚠ Thousands of simultaneous tests inflate false positives (test enough coincidences and some clear any fixed bar). The reason BH exists at all.
empirical / Monte-Carlo null⚠ The null distribution of T, estimated by sampling a benign population rather than by formula. calibrate.py: synth_benign → build_null

per-document vs estate-wide FDR⚠ BH's threshold is (k/m)·q; pool the whole estate and m explodes, so q/m → 0 and nothing survives at scale (155k files → zero). sift controls FDR per file. scan.py
π₀ (true-null proportion)⚠ Fraction of tests that are genuinely null. BH's actual FDR is π₀·q ≤ q, so π₀ says how conservative it really is. Not computed yet — see §5.
PRDS / independence⚠ BH's proof assumes the p-values are independent or positively dependent (PRDS). Candidates within one document may be neither — an assumption worth examining, not assuming.
precision · recall · TP/FP/FN○ Recall = caught / (caught + missed real). Precision = right / (right + false alarm). FDR tightening trades recall down for precision up.

The detectors — domain validators

TermWhat it is · why it matters · where it lives
Luhn checksum⚠ The mod-10 check on payment-card numbers (double every second digit from the right, sum, total ≡ 0 mod 10). About 1 in 10 random numbers pass — which is why a card's base = 0.1. sits.py: luhn_ok

base × context⚠ sift's coincidence-p-value model: structural coincidence rate (base) times contextual evidence (context). Sound as an ordering; only a real p-value after calibration (§4).

4 · The p-values are relative to the corpus

An empirical p-value only means "how often a coincidence in this population looks at least this sensitive." And coincidences differ by corpus:

  • a source repo is full of git SHAs, UUIDs, base64 blobs;
  • a legal archive is case numbers and dates;
  • a database export is sequential IDs and foreign keys.

So the calibrated p-values — and therefore which findings survive BH — depend on the estate being scanned. The shipped synthetic null is a generic model of "structured benign tokens"; for the FDR bound to hold on a given scan, the null should be built from a benign slice of that same estate (sift calibrate --corpus …, read locally, never committed). Validity is relative to how well the calibration population matches the scanned one — that relativity is the point, not a caveat.

This is what "materialized sketch" means precisely: the mechanism is concrete and runnable, but the numbers are only as real as the calibration corpus. The rung-2 synthetic default demonstrates the machinery; rung 3 (a per-estate null) is the version whose bound you would actually trust.

5 · The sketch — measuring the p-values BH consumes

The natural next study is to diagnose the distribution of the p-values feeding BH, rather than assert it is well-behaved. What that would contain:

  • The p-value histogram. Under a valid setup, true nulls are Uniform(0,1). So on benign data the histogram should be flat; on mixed data it should be a flat floor (nulls) plus a spike near 0 (true positives). The shape is the diagnostic — a non-flat null floor means the p-values are miscalibrated.
  • Estimate π₀ from the flat part of that histogram. Because BH's real FDR is π₀·q, π₀ tells you how much headroom you actually have (and lets you sharpen to an adaptive/Storey procedure if wanted).
  • The BH plot. Plot sorted p₍ₖ₎ against the line (k/m)·q; the crossing point is the decision. Seeing it shows how stable the threshold is — whether one candidate moving flips the cut.
  • The calibration curve. Generalize the existing super-uniformity test into a curve: empirical P(p ≤ α) versus α on held-out benign data. On the diagonal or below = valid; above = anti-conservative.
  • The dependence question (PRDS). Measure whether candidates within a document are independent enough for BH's guarantee, or whether the per-document pooling needs a dependence-robust variant (Benjamini–Yekutieli).

These are all measurements of the mechanism, computed on synthetic or local data — the same discipline as the rest of sift: state the property, then check it, rather than claim it.
