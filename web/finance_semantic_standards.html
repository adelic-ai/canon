FIBO / ACORD / XBRL

Finance & insurance already have the three-layer stratification canon found for cyber.

canon · domain parallel · 2026-06-05 · evidence the edge/join split is domain-general

The striking thing is not that finance has semantic standards — it is that it has the same three layers canon arrived at independently for cyber: an ontology (what things are, in RDF), a normalized exchange schema (the wire format, structured but not RDF), and a regulatory reporting layer. Two industries, one stratification — which is evidence the layering is a property of the problem, not of cyber.

The three standards

StandardWhat it isFormatLayer / role
FIBO Financial Industry Business Ontology (EDM Council + OMG) — instruments, legal entities, contracts, securities, derivatives, loans, modular by domain RDF / OWL the ontology — "what things are"
ACORD the insurance industry's data standards — policies, claims, parties, coverages (ACORD XML, the legacy AL3 EDI format, and the paper/PDF Forms) XML (RDF-mapping efforts exist) the exchange schema — insurance transactions on the wire
XBRL eXtensible Business Reporting Language — standardized financial facts for regulators (SEC filings, Solvency II for insurers); taxonomies + instance documents XML / iXBRL (semantically structured, RDF-mappable) the regulatory reporting layer
The parallel to cyber is exact. 
FIBO     ≈  CASE/UCO  / ATT&CK / D3FEND   — the RDF ontology (what entities ARE)
ACORD    ≈  OCSF                          — the normalized exchange schema (the wire format)
XBRL     ≈  compliance / audit reporting  — the regulatory-facts layer
Finance reached this stratification through decades of industry standardization; canon reached it through the self-validation architecture. Same three layers, arrived at independently — which supports the substrate being domain-agnostic, not cyber-shaped.

The edge/join decision generalizes

The same call canon made for OCSF-vs-RDF applies here unchanged: the XML standards (ACORD, XBRL) are the edge / wire format; FIBO is the graph you lift to at the join. Parse and normalize transactions and filings in ACORD/XBRL where their tooling lives; cross to the FIBO RDF graph at the point where you need identity merge, provenance, and cross-source reasoning. JSON/XML at the edge, RDF at the join — the principle is the same whether the domain is cyber or finance.

Why this matters for canon

  • Domain-general evidence. Two unrelated industries independently stratify into ontology + exchange-schema + reporting. That the split recurs is a point in favour of the substrate being a property of the problem (results computed from inputs that must justify themselves), not a cyber artifact.
  • FIBO is the borrow if canon takes on finance. It plays CASE/UCO's role — the RDF vocabulary for describing the domain's entities — and the cross-domain list already names finance / equities (anvil). The actuarial methods (credibility theory, EVT) go into Atlas as math; FIBO is the description layer, separately.
  • Same hybrid, same honesty. ACORD/XBRL at the edge keeps the mature XML tooling; FIBO at the join gives provenance + honest-NONE. Don't force filings through RDF; lift at the seam.
Confidence, stated. FIBO is genuinely RDF/OWL — high confidence. ACORD and XBRL are XML-native with RDF-mapping work — the existence of mappings is real, but verify the current state and any specific claim before citing.
