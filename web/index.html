The Sigma Rule Lattice: What the Corpus Actually Looks Likeresult The SKOS-graded lattice run over 332 rules (3 techniques): 2 true synonyms, ~22 subsumption pairs, 633 loose overlaps (96%). So Sigma doesn't dedup into clean categories — it's a graded relation graph. The actionable structure is the subsumption families (a general rule like "Non-Interactive PowerShell" → its stricter variants: ConvertTo-SecureString, CrackMapExec, base64…). Tables of the families (category→members) + the exact-match dedup classes. All structural; catch-set grounding would correct the edges. Per-TTP Coverage: Primitives, Atoms, Compositions, Catch-Classesnew A technique isn't covered by "≥1 rule" — it's a set of distinct checks, and the layer you count at decides what covered/redundant mean. Four recursive layers: primitive (count/sum/divide/log₂/…), atom/molecule (a Sigma clause OR a statistic like entropy = a molecule of primitives), rule (composition), catch-class (same instances = truth). Rules and the battery are atoms in ONE basis (match-ops + stat-ops); Sigma yaml is a partial frontend (fan-out's distinct-count is a Sigma value_count rule, its entropy isn't). Read the log once → the content-addressed atom-truth artifact → compose data-free. Independence is decided at the primitive grain by sub-DAG disjointness — distinct⟷entropy share the count-vector, so they're complementary, not corroborating.
