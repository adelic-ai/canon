D3FEND vs log-RDF — schema and data

Two kinds of RDF that meet, not the same thing in two places.

canon · ontology distinction · 2026-06-06 · T-Box / A-Box · joined by rdf:type

D3FEND and "RDF on the logs" are both RDF, but they are different kinds — the classic ontology split: T-Box vs A-Box (schema vs data, kinds vs instances). D3FEND describes what categories of things exist; the log-RDF asserts what specific things happened. They are complementary, and they meet by typing.

They meet via rdf:type. A log instance — a specific lsass.exe — is a d3f:Process; a specific Kerberos ticket is a d3f:ServiceTicket. So: 
D3FEND   defines (part of) the coordinate AXES   — the "what" / artifact-type axis
log-RDF  is the POINTS plotted in that space     — specific events
join     = rdf:type  (instance-of)               — the schema gives the points meaning
This is the "framework = coordinate system, graph = points" lens made precise: D3FEND's artifact ontology is the type-structure of the W-coordinates; the log-RDF populates it with occurrences.

Three consequences

  • You need both — not redundant RDF. The log says what happened; D3FEND says what kind it is and what defends against it. Detection is impossible with either alone — a coordinate system without points describes nothing real; points without a coordinate system are uninterpreted.
  • They're verified differently. The A-Box (log instances) is the new primary verification subject — custody / validity / provenance attach per event. The T-Box (D3FEND) is asserted knowledge — a validatable hypothesis, verified slowly and structurally against reality (the fidelity-attestation idea applied to the ontology), with its own source-provenance and tier. Verify the data; tier-and-use the schema.
  • Their reasoning lives at different layers. D3FEND's heavy OWL reasoning happens once, at the schema (materialize the type closure). The log-RDF then joins to the materialized types cheaply — fast traversal, no per-event DL inference. This resolves the SPARQL-closure-choke finding: reason once at the schema, not per event.
Caveat. D3FEND is the richest artifact-type ontology among the cyber frameworks — its deliberate contribution — so it best anchors the "what" axis. But the full coordinate system is D3FEND artifacts + CASE/UCO (instance-description) + ATT&CK data-components together, not D3FEND alone.
