The guarantee-tier ladder

Every verdict earns a tier from what actually held on its inputs — never asserts one. And the top rung isn't numeric pedantry: it's the defense against an adversary manipulating the computation in tolerances that sampling can't catch.

canon · session synthesis · 2026-06-17 · absent → well_formed → bounded → machine_checked · earned, not asserted

A canon verdict doesn't just say "anomaly." It says how strongly the claim is backed — and it computes that from what was actually true of this input, recording any demotion. The tiers form a ladder; a result climbs only as high as it earns.

The ladder, tersely. (higher = stronger; a result earns the meet of its sub-chain)
absent
No backing claim — a recorded absence, not a failure. The honest "we can't vouch."
well_formed
The structure is valid — SHACL-checkable provenance + property tests. The floor every detector earns. Earned by running a validator.
bounded
A distribution-free false-alarm bound (conformal, FAR ≤ α) — but only under exchangeability, gated by a runtime monitor. Where canon's real detection guarantee lives. Earned by running the monitor.
machine_checked
A formal proof — checked by a proof assistant — that the deterministic numeric skeleton meets its spec for all inputs (algebraic identities, IEEE-754 round-off bounds). Earned only by a proof artifact, never by running code.

1 · Earned, not asserted — and per result

The load-bearing rule: a tier is computed from a runtime check, recorded in provenance, and demotable. A claimed tier whose precondition didn't hold on this input is capped at the floor, and the demotion is written down (from_tier=…, the reason). Three of the four are now earned end-to-end:

well_formed     SHACL conformance of the verdict's PROV-O root        (validator runs)
bounded         exchangeability monitor confirms — no calibration drift (monitor runs)
                  → faker-kerberos (stationary): bounded
                  → flaws CloudTrail (drifting):  DEMOTED to well_formed  (refused, recorded)
machine_checked socket cut, proof absent → recorded absence → demote     (no proof artifact yet)

Custody and trustworthiness ride alongside as separate axes (earned by a signed-evidence attestation, or honestly parked when no feed signs its logs). The point everywhere: the substrate refuses a claim it can't back, and says so.

2 · Why machine_checked is gated

The lower tiers are earned by running a check — a SHACL validator, a KS monitor. machine_checked can only be earned by a proof artifact: a formal proof, checked by a proof assistant, over the whole input space. You cannot get that by running the kernel on examples — round-off behavior across all floats is exactly what sampling can't cover. So it's gated by two missing things:

  • The toolchain — proving round-off needs Coq/Rocq + Flocq + Gappa (round-off certificates), or F\*→Low\*→C extraction (HACL\*-style, where the executable is the proof). None wired into canon's build.
  • The proofs — even with the toolchain, each accumulation kernel (Welch averaging, CUSUM sum, Goertzel recurrence) must be formally proven. Specialized labor, not an afternoon.
The socket is cut; only the plug is missing. decode_guarantee_posture(decode, proof=NONE) already claims the ceiling (machine_checked); with no proof the monitor is a recorded absence → demote to well_formed, recording from_tier=MACHINE_CHECKED. Pass proof=TRUE the day a proof exists and it lifts — nothing else in the wiring changes. canon isn't blocked; it's refusing to claim the tier without the artifact — the same discipline as refusing bounded without the monitor.

3 · The top rung is not pedantry — it's an adversarial defense

"machine_checked is only the few accumulation kernels" is a benign-workload read. Under an adversarial threat model — canon's actual domain — it has a distinct, load-bearing purpose.

Sampled vs. all-inputs. The numeric parts of well_formed/bounded are sampled (property/metamorphic tests; a distributional bound). Nature draws at random, so sampling covers it. An adversary deliberately searches for the case you didn't sample — so property testing is evadable by construction. A machine-checked proof is over all inputs; it cannot be evaded that way. (Dijkstra: testing shows the presence of bugs, not their absence.) The top rung is the only tier that holds against an input an attacker chose, not one nature drew.

The attack class is minute, in-tolerance manipulation of the computation itself:

  • Threshold-boundary evasion — the detector checks stat > α; the float-computed stat differs from the exact real value by round-off ε. Craft inputs where the exact statistic is anomalous but the computed one lands just under α → evasion. Or the reverse, nudging benign data over → induced false alarms / alert-fatigue DoS. A proven ε lets you set the threshold tight (flag if computed > α − ε); an unproven ε leaves the window open.
  • Accumulation drift — CUSUM/Welch sums accrue float error over long runs; exploit the drift to slip a sequential detector.
  • Non-associativity — float addition isn't associative; an attacker who controls input order can shift a result. Algebraic-identity proofs close that.
Honest caveats. For noisy telemetry the round-off window (ε ~ 1e-15 relative) is swamped by measurement noise — there the pure round-off attack is mostly theoretical. It bites hardest where the adversary fully controls the input (crafted payloads, deterministic/structural checks) or where errors accumulate. And the proof stack is expensive — so the tier is cost-deferred, not value-deferred.

4 · This generalizes — high-assurance engineering and ICS/OT/SCADA

Wherever a computed value drives a consequential decision and an adversary (or fault) can hide in the gap between the idealized math and its floating-point implementation, the all-inputs proof is the defense. Cyber-physical control is the sharp case.

Stuxnet is the canonical example: minute in-tolerance manipulation (centrifuge frequencies) plus falsified telemetry to operators — physical harm engineered to stay under coarse alarms. That is exactly the threat the top rung addresses: not "is the detector right," but "can the computation be gamed within tolerance."

Safety-critical engineering already mandates this rigor at its top assurance levels — DO-178C Level A (avionics), IEC 61508 SIL 3/4 (functional safety), IEC 62443 (industrial) — using formal verification (SPARK/Ada, Frama-C, Coq/F\*) precisely because "tested" is insufficient when failure is physical. canon's machine_checked is that same culture, made a per-result, recorded, liftable tier rather than a one-time certification.

So the recalibration: machine_checked is not the least-valuable rung. It is the adversarial-robustness rung — the defense for when the attacker is gaming the detector's computation, not just hiding in the data. The "marginal/hair-splitting" read is correct only for benign workloads; the threat model flips the value. And it's central to the cyber-physical / OT applications canon could serve.
